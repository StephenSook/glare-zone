## Ghost lamp render order (trade-off)

Snap's Design Best Practices advise against a high render order for virtual objects far from the user: far content drawn over near content reads as nearer and causes discomfort. The ghost lamp is the lamp's mirror image behind the wall, seen through the glass exactly where the glare spot is on the print. A depth-tested ghost lamp is hidden by the opaque print by construction, because the line from the eye to the ghost passes through the spot on the print. So the ghost lamp keeps depth testing off and render order 50. To limit the discomfort it is faint (alpha 0.25, was 0.45; LEAF C4 checks it stays under 0.3) and drawn only while the eye is in a glare zone (C4 checks it fades out on stepping out). The lamp-to-spot ray lies in front of the wall, so it now keeps depth testing and the default render order. A depth-tested ghost lamp was not tried in the preview; the reason above is geometric.

## Known limits

- The Q and E crouch keys were not pressed by hand. Eye-height changes were driven with MovePreviewCamera only.
- Preview coordinate pinches did not trigger the near-panel buttons (More controls); the LEAF interactor presses every control reliably.
- The near panels follow position only. If you turn around, they stay behind you until you raise your left palm and press "Bring panels here".
- The palm-up detection runs only on a device with hand tracking; in the preview the button appears only through `showAt()`.
- IK reach (C19, fail-fast): LEAF's IK arm presses the near controls by ray and the three palm menu buttons by SIK's physical proximity (row 24). Pressing within reach needs the test-only `bridgeLeafDirectPoke` (Leaf 2.0.2 with this project's SIK, runtime 0.18.0: SIK binds its poke functions in the provider's constructor, so LEAF's replacements are never called and no press within reach hovers, for any Interactable). The Lens itself is unchanged by it. A newer LEAF needs a newer SIK (row 2). LEAF records a hover or trigger timeout without throwing, even under fail-fast, so C19 asserts after every press that no reach diagnostic was recorded. C19 needs `__leaf__diagnostic_policy` set to fail-fast in the run parameters.
- Wall placement: LEAF cannot make a free-space pinch, so C13 calls the placer's pinch handler (`confirm()`) instead of pinching; the pinch-release detection itself is untested. C13 needs a preview scene with walls (Sunlit Room): the Plane scene returns no World Query hit at all. The placed print hangs at its real height above the practice floor, which is set by the chosen eye height, not by the height of the hit. Not tried on a SPECS device.
- The MCP tool's time limit is shorter than most scenarios plus the Lens reset: wait for each run's PASSED or FAILED line in the log before sending the next call, or the next run resets the Lens and cuts the running one short (C8 and C17 in row 27).
- Lamp model (row 26): each light uses the TSLight RXT13's published centre-beam candlepower (2,700 cd with the 40 deg reflector, 5,000 cd with the 20 deg) everywhere inside a hard-edged cone of the beam angle, and nothing outside it. A real beam falls off toward its edge and spills past it, so levels near the beam's edge read high and spill is ignored. Manufacturer data, not measured.
- The RXT13 sheet rates the fixture to 45 deg from vertical ("Maximum Operating Angle"); the Lens lets a light tilt to 80 deg and does not warn past 45 deg. Several scenarios tilt a light to 80 deg to take it off the glass.
- The More column gap follows the aim handle from the panels' eye point; for a "My eyes" height far from the presets the gap is kept inside the column's space and may no longer line up with the handle. Checked at the four presets only.
- The floor labels are placed for the panels' eye point, so they read best from where the panels are; walking up to the wall leaves them where they were until the panels follow.
- "All three" speaks for 160, 109 and 104 cm. A lower eye is covered only while "My eyes" is chosen and the eye is that low (row 29). Wheelchair users' eye heights go down to 96 cm (UK Inclusive Mobility, 5th percentile); at the 62 cm demo setback a 96 cm eye sees glare to 64.48 cm, and only lights under 57.69 cm clear it.
- Frame shadows are not modelled (row 28): closer lights cast a longer shadow of the frame's top rail on the print (100 x d / (D - d) cm in this room), and the Lens does not show it.
- The clear cue is checked with `isPlaying()` in the preview (C23); its loudness (volume 0.35 on a peak-normalized WAV) was not judged by ear, here or on a device.
- The frame's forward lean (row 36) is modelled and drawn but has no on-screen control: it is set through the controller (LEAF). The glare spot is drawn flat at its point on the leaning glass.
- With the default 40 deg reflector the two lamps' lobes overlap almost entirely at the standard placement, so the per-lamp patches read as one soft-edged patch; they part visibly with the 20 deg reflector (row 36).
- The wheelchair estimate (row 37) assumes a normal distribution of eye height fitted to the DfT's 5th and 95th percentiles (960 and 1250 mm); the guide gives the two percentiles only. It counts eyes whose zone reaches the viewing area, as the card does; it is an estimate, not a survey.
- The results view (Save results) lists each light's own distances, so a distance can show there more than once (per light); every other screen shows each glare distance once (C27).
- The beam angle label (row 40) is the beam axis from the horizontal; sliding a light keeps its tilt, so the label changes only when the light is re-aimed.
